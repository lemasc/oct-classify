# AdaBN on ResNet-50 LOSO Checkpoints

## Scope and Provenance

This is experiment 2 in [cross-device-plan.md](../cross-device-plan.md). It asks whether re-estimating
BatchNorm statistics on the held-out source's unlabeled images (AdaBN) closes the leave-one-source-out
(LOSO) gap. Weights are not retrained. All 16 median-padding LOSO checkpoints
(`fused/resnet50/loso-*-local-20261001-132356*`) were re-evaluated on their held-out source's full
manifest in two variants:

| Variant | BatchNorm layers re-estimated | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `adabn-all` | All 53 | `evaluations/<source>-loso-adabn-all/` |
| `adabn-early` | Stem `bn1` and `layer1` (11 of 53) | `evaluations/<source>-loso-adabn-early/` |

```bash
uv run oct-classify train evaluate --checkpoint <run>/checkpoint-best.pt --source <held-out> \
    --split-dir artifacts/splits --full-source --adapt-bn --adapt-bn-layers {all,early} \
    --output <run>/evaluations/<held-out>-loso-adabn-{all,early} --device cuda
uv run python .scratchpad/7fc25cf_adabn/score_adabn.py
```

The sweep took 95 minutes on the RTX 3060 (`.scratchpad/7fc25cf_adabn/sweep.sh`); about 80 of those
were spent on held-out Kermany.

## Method

- **Patient cross-fit.** The held-out source's patients are split into two halves, stratified by
  each patient's majority label (seed 20261004). Statistics come from one half and are used to predict
  the other, then the halves swap. No image is classified with statistics from its own patient, and
  every image gets an adapted prediction, so results pair row for row with the unadapted predictions.
- **Statistics.** Each adapted BatchNorm layer is reset and refilled with a cumulative average
  (`momentum=None`) over shuffled, unaugmented batches that use the checkpoint's own normalization.
  Learned weights do not change.
- **Scoring.** Fold-averaged metrics with 95% patient-level cluster-bootstrap intervals, and a
  **paired** AdaBN-minus-unadapted difference: each replicate resamples patients once and scores both
  variants on the same draw. The binary view is the Normal-vs-AMD rescoring from
  [20261004-binary-rescore.md](20261004-binary-rescore.md).

### Implementation checks

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Unit tests (`tests/test_bn_adaptation.py`) | Statistics equal the target statistics; weights unchanged; patient groups kept whole and stratified |
| Refactored evaluation with `--adapt-bn` off vs. saved held-out-OCTDL fold-1 predictions | Identical (max probability difference 0.0) |
| Held-out-OCTDL fold-1 model scored on Kermany's test split (a source it trained on), macro-F1 | Unadapted 97.6; `adabn-all` on Kermany alone 91.6; `adabn-all` on a balanced mix of its 3 training sources 95.3 |

The last row shows that the code recomputes statistics correctly: statistics from a mix of the training
sources mostly restore performance. Statistics from a single source hurt even where there is no domain shift.

## Interpretation

- **AdaBN does not close the gap.** The best case is `adabn-all` on held-out Kermany: +1.1 macro-F1
  and +1.7 AUROC, mostly from DME recall (65.5 to 78.6). That is small next to the 22-point
  gap. PAIMA, the one source with a real Normal-vs-AMD shift, moves by less than 1 point.
- **On OCTDL it is harmful.** Both variants cost about 18-20 macro-F1 points, and predictions move into
  DME (DME recall rises to 91-96 while normal and AMD recall fall to 74-83). The binary view barely
  moves (-1.4), so the damage is concentrated in the DME output. This matches experiment 1: on unseen
  devices, the DME decision is the fragile one.
- **Kermany's lower normal recall is not caused by BatchNorm statistics.** Binary normal recall stays
  at 89 under both variants. The decision-cutoff shift found in experiment 1 is not a shift in feature
  statistics that re-estimating them can undo.
- **Adapting only the early layers does not help.** Restricting adaptation to the stem and `layer1`
  was meant to correct low-level intensity differences while leaving the class-dependent deep
  statistics alone. It is no better than `adabn-all` anywhere in 3-class and is worse on Kermany and
  Duke. So the harm is not explained by deep layers picking up the target's class mix.
- **These models are a poor fit for AdaBN.** They trained on batches balanced across three sources and
  across classes, so their BatchNorm statistics describe that mix. The check on Kermany, a training
  source, already showed that single-source statistics cost 6 points without any device shift.
  Training-time options (experiment 3, augmentation) or per-source normalization during training
  ("domain-conditioned normalization" in the project plan's §6) fit this setup better.

## Caveats

- Held-out Duke is a single model scored on 45 eyes. Its +2.4 (`adabn-all`, 3-class) and -3.8
  (`adabn-early`) are within the noise expected from one model.
- Kermany and OCTDL intervals are narrow because they average 5 checkpoints over many patients. They
  measure uncertainty from sampling patients, not from training randomness.
- The cross-fit uses half the source for each estimate (about 50k images for Kermany, about 850 for OCTDL).
  OCTDL's statistics are therefore noisier, but the effect is too large and too consistent across
  5 checkpoints to be estimation noise.
- AdaBN uses the target's unlabeled images. That is a different setting from pure LOSO, where no
  target image is seen.
